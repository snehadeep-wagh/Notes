**Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)** is the "marriage" of two concepts: **Information Retrieval** (fetching relevant data) and **Text Generation** (LLMs producing human-like responses). It makes an LLM smarter by providing it with specific, extra information at the moment you ask a question.

### **1. Why RAG? (The Core Problems Solved)**

Directly prompting an LLM fails in three specific scenarios that RAG addresses:

- **Private Data:** LLMs haven't seen your internal company emails or private video transcripts during training.
- **Knowledge Cutoff:** Models have a "cutoff date" (e.g., First pre-trained on Jan 1st) and cannot answer questions about current news or events after that date.
- **Hallucination:** Without context, LLMs might confidently invent incorrect answers. RAG "grounds" the model by forcing it to answer only from the provided text.

### **2. The Historical Evolution**

To understand RAG, we must look at how AI learning evolved:

1. **Pre-training:** Learning from the entire internet to gain "Parametric Knowledge" (stored in weights).
2. **Fine-tuning:** Re-training a model on a smaller, domain-specific dataset (expensive and requires labeled data).
3. **In-Context Learning:** Providing examples in the prompt (Few-Shot Prompting) so the model learns a task without weight updates.
4. **RAG:** Instead of just examples, we provide the **entire relevant context** needed to solve a specific query.

### **3. The 4-Step RAG Pipeline**

A RAG system follows a specific workflow to turn raw data into an answer.

##### **Step 1: Indexing (Building the Knowledge Base)**
Indexingis the process of preparing your knowledge base so that it can be efficiently searchedat query time. This steps consists of 4 sub-steps.

Indexing prepares your data so it can be searched efficiently.

- **Document Ingestion:** Loading data from sources like YouTube, PDFs, or URLs into memory.

![[Pasted image 20260803173909.png]]

- **Text Chunking:** Breaking large documents into small, semantically meaningful "chunks" to fit within the LLM's context limit.

![[Pasted image 20260803173953.png|700]]
![[Pasted image 20260803174158.png]]

- **Embedding Generation:** Converting text chunks into "Dense Vectors" (embeddings) that capture their numerical meaning.

![[Pasted image 20260803174238.png]]
![[Pasted image 20260803174301.png]]

- **Vector Store:** Saving these vectors and their original text into a specialized database (like Chroma or FAISS).

![[Pasted image 20260803174337.png]]
![[Pasted image 20260803174418.png]]


##### **Step 2: Retrieval**
Retrieval is the real-time process of finding the most relevant pieces of information from a pre-built index (created during indexing) based on the user’s question.

When a user asks a question, the system searches the Vector Store for the top-k (usually 3 to 5) most relevant chunks.

![[Pasted image 20260803174651.png]]
##### **Step 3: Augmentation**

Augmentation refers to the step where the retrieved documents (chunks of relevant context) are combined with the user’s query to form a new, enriched prompt for the LLM.

The system merges the **User Query** with the **Retrieved Context** into a single prompt template.

![[Pasted image 20260803175448.png]]


##### **Step 4: Generation**
Generation is the final step where a Large Language Model (LLM) uses the user’s query and the retrieved & augmented context to generate a response.

The LLM reads the combined prompt and generates a response based _only_ on the provided context.

![[Pasted image 20260803175547.png]]

### **4. RAG Performance & Evaluation**

To ensure a RAG system is accurate, we use the **Ragas** framework to measure four key metrics:

|Metric|What it Measures|
|---|---|
|**Faithfulness**|Is the answer grounded solely in the retrieved context?|
|**Answer Relevancy**|Does the answer actually address the user's specific question?|
|**Context Precision**|How much of the retrieved context is actually useful?|
|**Context Recall**|Did the system retrieve all the necessary info to answer the question?|
